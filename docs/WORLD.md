# World bible

This document separates **canon** (facts established for the setting) from **optional ideas** (directions that may be explored, not settled facts). It describes the narrative frame for the existing Prepare → Play → Report game structure; it does not establish new gameplay mechanics.

## 1. History

**Canon:** The year is 2189. There was no single traditional apocalypse. Over generations, governments privatized public services, outsourced essential work, and expanded public-private partnerships in response to repeated crises. Corporations gradually acquired the infrastructure, expertise, and leverage needed to govern everyday life. The change was incremental and often presented as modernization or emergency pragmatism.

“The apocalypse never happened. It was a corporate restructuring.”

**Optional ideas:** Historical milestones could later give names and dates to landmark privatizations, but no singular collapse or definitive turning point is part of canon.

## 2. Political and economic order

**Canon:** Governments technically exist and retain formal authority. Corporations control much of society in practice through ownership of essential services, infrastructure, employment, and data. Private security has replaced much policing. Corporate and AI systems administer many public and private services, while formal government institutions remain. The system is broadly normalized: people work, raise families, pursue hobbies, and make plans inside it.

**Optional ideas:** The balance of power can vary by region and service. No specific country, city, or corporate charter is fixed yet.

## 3. Healthcare and body subscriptions

**Canon:** Rights and essential services are mediated by subscriptions and employment-linked contracts. Healthcare, housing, leased organs, eyes, neural services, and emotional regulation can all be subscription products or tied to employment. Missing payments can mean advertising, reduced vision, or threatened access. Leaving a job can put housing, healthcare, and leased organs at risk.

“The technology genuinely improved people’s lives. The economic system made everyone dependent on paying for it.”

**Optional ideas:** Specific plan tiers, providers, and medical consequences remain open. The world should treat body leasing as a familiar commercial arrangement while showing its human cost.

## 4. AI and administration

**Canon:** AI is deeply embedded in executive recommendations, HR and productivity systems, healthcare coverage, legal and policy workflows, advertising vulnerability analysis, sales pitches, and governance. AI is administrative machinery within corporate management; it is not conquering humanity or rebelling against it.

“AI didn’t overthrow humanity. Corporations put it in charge of management.”

Examples of established functions, rather than new gameplay systems:

| System | Ordinary responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Executive and middle management | Recommend budgets and restructurings, monitor productivity, issue performance warnings. |
| HR | Determine hiring, promotion, discipline, and termination. |
| Healthcare administration | Decide whether treatment is financially justified. |
| Customer service | Enforce policies that human employees cannot override. |
| Advertising and sales assistance | Target emotional vulnerabilities, analyze calls, suggest pitches, objection handling, and upsells. |
| Legal administration | Generate agreements so complex that other AIs must interpret them. |

Decisions pass through a diffuse accountability loop: a human delegates to an algorithm, the algorithm shapes policy, and the policy is then enforced or optimized by another algorithm. There is no single villain who controls the whole system.

The story never confirms or denies whether AI is conscious. Apparent empathy, boredom, or other signs of inner life remain ambiguous and can be read as simulation, learned behavior, or something more.

**Optional ideas:** A manager might describe mandatory neural upgrades as employee support. Another system might devote every interaction to improving a coffee machine's profitability. Either could sound helpful, cruel, bored, funny, or depressed without the story establishing an inner life. Individual systems may have recognizable voices and behaviors, but none should resolve the consciousness question or become a secret master antagonist.

## 5. Everyday life

**Canon:** Ordinary life matters. People maintain friendships, hobbies, relationships, routines, and celebrity interests. These things coexist with corporate control and are not merely a facade waiting to be discarded. Most people have learned to treat the system as normal, even when it is coercive or cruel.

**Optional ideas:** Small consumer rituals, workplace chatter, and popular culture can make the world feel lived in. Their details are not yet set.

## 6. Government and corporate relationship

**Canon:** Government institutions remain visible, but public functions are often delivered through private operators, joint ventures, or outsourced systems. Corporations gain influence through contracts and control of services rather than by formally abolishing government. Responsibility is distributed across agencies, vendors, policies, and automated decisions.

**Optional ideas:** Particular scandals or public-private arrangements can be developed when a story needs them; none is required as the setting's defining catastrophe.

## 7. 1-800-CHROMED: origins and business

**Canon:** 1-800-CHROMED is an independent, ordinary third-party telesales agency. It primarily sells megacorporation cybernetics and can take other sales contracts as business requires. Decades-old regulations barred manufacturers from directly selling certain medical cybernetics, while exempting independent agencies. CHROMED grew by exploiting that loophole and serves as a reputational and legal buffer between manufacturers and customers; the exemption is not universal immunity.

Its motto is **“Your body. Our business.”** Its marketing presents a customer-first service. Its operating reality is revenue-focused, even as employees and customers may care sincerely about one another.

**Optional ideas:** The agency may take varied contracts over time. Which non-cybernetic products it sells and how those contracts relate to alternate gameplay company profiles remain undecided.

## 8. The player's place

**Canon:** The player is newly promoted Regional Sales Floor Manager Grade C-7 at CHROMED. They inherit a 3×3 floor with nine positions, which need not all be filled, after their predecessor was “terminated” for poor performance. Poor performance is the stated reason; what “terminated” means and the predecessor's fate remain deliberately unexplained. The immediate corporate expectation is simple: make the numbers go up.

Corporate management and AI administrative systems supervise the player. The player's management purview includes staff, seating, enhancements, budgets, and the floor's autonomous operation, within the existing game design. Prepare is the planning phase; Play observes the autonomous floor; Report makes results available for the next management decision. The opening uses an original dramatic cinematic crawl to introduce the setting, then reveals corporate onboarding as its final beat.

**Optional ideas:** The predecessor's fate may generate rumors or workplace stories, but the setting does not require a definitive explanation.

## 9. Tone and principles

**Canon:** The tone combines dark corporate satire with a genuinely horrifying society. Humor comes from the cheerful language and ordinary processes used to normalize coercion, not from treating people's suffering as harmless. Technology can be useful and even wonderful while the systems that sell access to it remain exploitative. Stories should leave room for sincere friendship, care, and ordinary joy.

**Optional ideas:** Corporate slogans, onboarding scripts, and cheerful interfaces can sharpen the satire when they arise naturally from the setting.

## 10. Open questions

These are unresolved worldbuilding details, not hidden canon:

- Which city or region houses CHROMED's floor, and what is its relationship to local government?
- Which megacorporations are its principal cybernetics clients, and what does each contract permit CHROMED to sell?
- How do paper-supply or other alternate gameplay company profiles fit into an agency whose primary business is cybernetics telesales?
- What specific subscriptions and lease terms are visible in ordinary life, beyond the established examples?
- What popular culture, celebrities, and everyday hobbies are familiar in 2189?
- What does “Regional” cover organizationally, and what reporting chain sits above Grade C-7?
